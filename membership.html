The Pink Mountain Hike

The alarm goes off before the city does. You pull into the trailhead parking lot as the sky is deciding what color it wants to be. A few cars already there. Someone hands you a coffee before you have said a word. A pastry wrapped in paper. You do not know everyone yet. That changes on the way up.

This morning has been timed to the minute. When the light arrives, it finds the highest ridgeline first. Rose deepens into coral. Coral bleeds into amber. The light travels downward across the range, the washes far below the last to receive it.

It happens almost every morning in Palm Springs. Most people are asleep for it. Most who are awake are not here for it.

You feel it on your skin before you feel it anywhere else. The person next to you is smiling. So are you.

By the time you are back at the trailhead, you have made plans for the next one.

The Summer Table

Summer in Palm Springs has a different pace. The calendar slows. The weekends open up. And on one night this season, members will sit down together at one of Palm Springs' best restaurants for a dinner that does not appear on any public calendar.

Inside, where it is cool and unhurried, a menu built for the occasion. A long table. The kind of conversation that starts before the first course arrives because everyone at it has been somewhere this season and nobody needs context.

People who know this place in a way that takes time to earn. Who have found a different relationship with the desert when the rest of the world is not watching.

The summer table exists because some of the best evenings in Palm Springs happen when nobody is in a hurry to be anywhere else.

A Few Times a Season

The Happy Hour

Not every Club night needs a reservation. A few times a season, members gather for something looser. A bar takeover. A rooftop. A space that fits thirty people if everyone is willing to stand close.

Palm Springs has a new social energy moving through it and PSAC members are at the center of it. This is where new members meet the ones who have been here since the beginning. Where someone overhears a trail recommendation that changes their next weekend. Where a guest pass gets used and the person who shows up realizes halfway through the evening that they need to be a member.

Show up. See who is there. That is the whole plan.

Member Only Events

The Marquee Experience

Some experiences require more than showing up. These are the ones members talk about for years.

Cactus to Clouds in a single day. Fourteen miles. Ten thousand feet of gain from the desert floor to the summit of San Jacinto. The kind of effort that changes how you see the valley below.

A Desert Riders breakfast at dawn, followed by the trails those riders first explored almost a century ago. The valley floor spread out below and the city not yet awake.

Three days in the San Jacinto wilderness. Up the Skyline Trail. South on the PCT. Down Jo Pond. Nights deep in the pines. The version of this place almost nobody sees.

Every marquee experience is member-only. Logistics handled. Permits secured. Safety plan in place. And when it is over, a keepsake that marks what the group did together.

These do not appear on the public calendar. They open to members first, fill quickly, and do not come back around until the season is right again.
