Discover Palm Springs

There is more here than most people find.

Palm Springs is not what it appears to be from the outside. The Cahuilla people have known this valley for centuries. Cary Grant had an estate here. The Desert Riders rode trails into these mountains that most visitors never find — trails largely belonging to the Cahuilla, ridden with their permission and guidance beginning in 1930. There are no high-rises. No chain stores. No paparazzi. No drones. This place made rules to protect something worth protecting — and most people never discover what that something is.

Palm Springs Adventure Club exists to show you what most people miss.

Twice Monthly

The Pink Mountain Hike

The alarm goes off before the city does. Someone hands you a coffee before you have said a word. You do not know everyone yet. That changes on the way up.

This morning has been timed to the minute. When the light arrives, it finds the highest ridgeline first. Rose deepens into coral. Coral bleeds into amber.

It happens almost every morning in Palm Springs. Most people are asleep for it.

By the time you are back at the trailhead, you have made plans for the next one.

Members Only

The Summer Table

Summer in Palm Springs has a different pace. And on one night this season, members will sit down together at one of Palm Springs' best restaurants for a dinner that does not appear on any public calendar.

Inside, where it is cool and unhurried, a menu built for the occasion. A long table. The kind of conversation that starts before the first course arrives because nobody needs context.

The best evenings in Palm Springs happen when nobody is in a hurry to be anywhere else.

A Few Times a Season

The Happy Hour

Not every Club night needs a reservation. A bar takeover. A rooftop. A space that fits thirty people if everyone is willing to stand close.

This is where new members meet the ones who have been here since the beginning. Where someone overhears a trail recommendation that changes their next weekend. Where a guest pass gets used and the person who shows up realizes they need to be a member.

Show up. See who is there. That is the whole plan.

Member Only Events

The Marquee Experience

Some experiences require more than showing up. These are the ones members talk about for years.

Cactus to Clouds in a single day. A Desert Riders breakfast at dawn on the trails those riders first explored a century ago. Three days in the San Jacinto wilderness — up the Skyline Trail, south on the PCT, down Jo Pond. Nights deep in the pines.

Every marquee experience is member-only. Logistics handled. Permits secured. A keepsake when it is over.

They open to members first, fill quickly, and do not come back around until the season is right again.
