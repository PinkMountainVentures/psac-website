01 · The Opening
01
Your day.

Congrats on ten years! That's a decade of choosing each other, and you're here, in one of the most beautiful places in the world, celebrating. We're genuinely glad you found us and we want this day to be worthy of what it's marking.

You asked for something that feels like it belongs to you. Something you couldn't have found on your own. That's exactly what we're going to give you.

One honest thought before we get into it: West Fork is on your list and we love that. It's also nearly ten miles, close to 3,000 feet of gain, and the descent has sections that a knee will remember the next day. That's a big ask for an anniversary. We think we have something better for today, but if West Fork is calling you, say the word and we'll build that guide instead.

What we'd love to show you is the Bogert-Berns Loop in the Santa Rosa Mountains. Five miles, about 1,268 feet of gain, and you'll be back at Colony Palms before noon with the whole afternoon still ahead of you. There's a canyon wash at the start that smells of desert lavender on a cool November morning. A ridgeline with San Jacinto lit up behind you and views across Palm Springs, including Bob Hope's famous hilltop home and the Palm Springs Airport. The loop will likely be entirely yours, because almost nobody finds it.

If you're going with Bogert-Berns, and we think you'll love it, read on.

02 · The Terrain
02
The place.

The Bogert trailhead sits at the edge of where Palm Springs ends and the Santa Rosa Mountains begin. A dirt parking lot, a Desert Riders trail sign, and then the mountain in front of you asking what you came for. The air at 762 feet in mid-November carries something the resort air doesn't. Cooler. Drier. Faintly scented with sage and something older underneath it. The city is still visible behind you but already it feels like it belongs to a different part of the day.

The trail begins as a dirt road. Wide, unhurried, giving you time to settle into your legs and your breathing before it asks anything serious of you. At a third of a mile the road drops into a wash and the world narrows. High rock walls rise on both sides. Flowering plants crowd the trail edges. If you've timed your morning right the sun is just reaching the canyon floor and the dew is still sitting on the leaves. The fragrance that rises from that combination is something you won't find described anywhere because most people move through it too quickly to notice. Stop here. Breathe it in. This is the first gift of the morning.

This is not a trail many people find unless they are local. The Santa Rosa Mountains have a character that's different from the drama of 10,000+ foot San Jacinto to the north. More weathered, gentler, and absolutely beautiful. They'll help you find your pace while you focus on each other and enjoy the views.

Follow the road up from the wash. The mountain takes it from here.
03 · Your Route
03
The way.
Bogert-Berns Loop
Distance
5.18 miles
Elevation Gain
1,268 ft
Duration
2.5 to 3 hours
Type
Loop
Mile
0
The Trailhead
Park in the dirt lot at the end of Bogert Trail Road. Look for the iron Desert Riders trail sign at the far end of the lot. Take a moment here before you move. The city is behind you. The mountains are in front of you. This is the transition.
Miles
0–0.5
The Approach and The Wash
Follow the dirt road beyond the trail sign. The grade is gentle and the footing is easy. Let your legs warm up and your breathing settle. At 0.5 miles the road drops into the wash. The walls rise on both sides. Flowering plants crowd the edges. Stop before you cross. The desert lavender warming in the morning sun with overnight dew still on the leaves produces a fragrance you won't find described anywhere because most people move through it too quickly to notice. Breathe it in. This moment won't last long. Cross the wash, turn a sharp left, and rejoin the road on the other side.
Miles
0.72–1.2
The Single Track Climb
The trail transitions from dirt road to single track and the switchbacks begin. This section asks something real of you. The footing is loose in places and the grade is consistent. Find a pace you can both hold comfortably and stay there. This is not a section to push. Talk if you want to. Or don't. The trail is good company on its own. At the top of the switchbacks stop and turn around. The San Jacinto range fills the view behind you, morning light warming its face. At over 10,000 feet it is an extraordinary thing to look at. Take your time here. You've earned this already and the best of the trail is still ahead.
Miles
1.2–1.6
Twists, Turns and the Wildhorse Junction
The trail curves and winds before arriving at the junction with the Wildhorse Trail. Continue straight through the junction. The trail bends left and the terrain opens up around you.
Mile
1.78
The T Intersection
You'll arrive at a T intersection with no markings. Take a right. This is the turn most people miss. Trust it.
Mile
2.1
Stay on the Dirt Road
The trail arrives at what appears to be a dirt road bending left. Do not take the single track trail to the right. Follow the dirt road up and to the left. The summit is ahead of you.
Mile
2.42
The Overlook
The views open fully here. Palm Springs spreads out below you. Bob Hope's former residence sits perched on its hilltop, visible and unmistakable. The Palm Springs Airport lies beyond it, the valley floor stretching out in the morning light. This is also where the Araby Trail tops out, though you won't see many people arriving from that direction either. You are unlikely to share this spot with anyone. Stay as long as you want. This is one of the quieter places in the desert.
Optional Side Quest — Cahuilla Hills Peak, 1,522 ft
A trail continues straight toward the next summit. Follow it to the top where you'll find a cairn built stone by stone by everyone who has made it this far. Add yours if you like. Then turn around and return to mile 2.42 before continuing the loop. Do not continue past the cairn.
Mile
2.46
The Berns Trail
Veer left onto the Berns Trail. The switchbacks descend into the valley below. The footing requires attention here, particularly on the steeper sections. Take your time on the descent and let the knee set the pace. There is no rush and the trail will reward patience.
Mile
2.73
The Quiet Wash
At the bottom of the valley you'll cross a wash. This is one of the more peaceful spots on the entire loop. The views open toward the Palm Springs Airport and the valley beyond. Few people ever stand here. It has a stillness to it in the morning that is worth pausing for, even briefly. From the wash the trail climbs again through another set of switchbacks on the opposite side of the valley.
Mile
3.16
Unmarked Left Turn
At the top of the switchbacks you'll reach a trail intersection. Take the left turn onto the unmarked trail. This is easy to miss. Look for it before you assume the trail continues straight.
Mile
3.31
Garstin Trail Junction
Take another left onto the Garstin Trail.
Mile
3.48
Stay Right
Stay right as the trail descends toward the valley below. This puts you back toward the Wildhorse and Bogert junction.
Mile
3.56
Back to Bogert
Turn right onto the Bogert Trail. From here retrace your steps back down the switchbacks, through the canyon wash and its lavender fragrance one more time, and along the dirt road back to the parking lot.
Mile
5.18
The Parking Lot
On the way down the San Jacinto range will be fully lit behind you, the morning well underway. You'll arrive back at your vehicle having seen something most visitors to Palm Springs never find, and with the whole day still ahead of you.
04 · The Moments
04
What to slow down for.
Mile 0.5
The Lavender Wash
Stop at the wash before you cross. The desert lavender warming in the early sun with overnight dew still on the leaves produces a fragrance that is entirely specific to this place and this hour. It won't be here when you return in the afternoon. This one belongs to the morning.
Mile 1.2
San Jacinto Viewpoint
The trail flattens here after the switchback climb and the San Jacinto range appears in full. At over 10,000 feet it doesn't just fill the view — it overwhelms it. Indian Canyons spreads below, the southern end of Palm Springs visible beyond it, the scale of the mountain above everything. In morning light the range is glorious. This is a place to slow down together and simply look. The trail will wait.
Mile 2.42
The Overlook
Turn one direction and San Jacinto fills the sky. Turn the other and the entire Coachella Valley opens to the horizon, Palm Springs below you, the valley stretching east toward the Little San Bernardino Mountains, the desert floor glowing in the morning light. This is a place where the scale of things becomes suddenly and quietly apparent. Almost nobody stands here. Take your time.
05 · Prepare Well
05
Before you go.
Footwear
Trail Running Shoes
Leave the hiking boots at home if you don't wear them regularly. Stiff boots on unfamiliar feet create more problems than they solve on a trail like this. What works best here is a trail running shoe with good grip and a protective outsole. Altra Lone Peak, Hoka Speedgoat, and Nike Trail runners are all excellent choices. If ankle support matters, Altra makes a mid-boot version that gives you stability without the weight and stiffness of a traditional hiking boot.
Gear
Trekking Poles
Worth having, particularly for the Berns Trail descent. If you don't own a pair, we rent them. The loose footing on the downhill section is manageable without them, but poles give the knee something to lean on and take real load off the legs over five miles. If you'd like to add them to your day, reply to this email and we'll have them ready for you.
Hydration
Water and Electrolytes
Two liters each, minimum. There is no water on this trail. Even on a cool November morning the elevation gain will have you working, and the desert air is drier than most people expect. Drink before you feel thirsty. Add a quality electrolyte packet to your water — it makes a meaningful difference on the back half of the trail. If you rented gear from us, electrolytes are already in your bag.
Food
Trail Snacks
Eat before you feel like you need to. The elevation gain will have you burning more than a morning walk. Simple whole foods work best — nuts, dates, a banana, some jerky. All easily found at any grocery store on the way. If you rented gear from us, snacks are already in your bag.
Clothing
What to Wear
Expect temperatures in the low to mid fifties at the trailhead, warming through the sixties as the morning progresses. A light layer at the start that can tie around your waist by mile one is ideal. Sun protection matters even in November — sunscreen, sunglasses, and a hat.
Personal
A Note for James
The steepest and loosest section of the entire loop is the Berns Trail descent from mile 2.46 to the wash. Short steps, weight back slightly, slow and deliberate pace through here. Poles make this section significantly easier. The quiet valley and the rest of the loop are just ahead.

06 · If the Day Changes
06
When to adjust.

This trail is well within your reach and November mornings in the Santa Rosas are forgiving. That said, a few things worth knowing before you head out.

The Knee
If James's knee becomes genuinely painful rather than just present, stop and assess before continuing. Retracing back down the Bogert Trail to the parking lot is always the simplest option from any point on the loop. If you've reached the overlook at mile 2.42 or beyond, the Araby Trail descends from just past that point into the southern end of Palm Springs, and the main dirt road further along the loop connects down to Highway 111 — both are reasonable alternatives. Uber can get you back to your car at the Bogert trailhead from either exit point.
Weather
Check the forecast the night before. November in Palm Springs is reliably clear but the mountains can surprise. If clouds are building unusually fast or the wind picks up significantly at the overlook, head back toward the trailhead rather than continuing the loop.
If You Need Help
Cell service is generally good on this trail. In an emergency call 911. If signal is weak, try 760-836-3220, the Riverside County Sheriff's desert dispatch line. Share your planned route and expected return time with the hotel concierge before you leave — a simple text works fine.
07 · After the Trail
07
The other half of
your day.

You'll be back at Colony Palms before noon. The whole afternoon is yours.

1
First — The Spa
Book the Colony Palms Spa before you go out on the trail. Not after. Treatments fill up and an anniversary deserves a guaranteed afternoon rather than a waitlist.
Walk through the garden when you arrive. Stop at the fountain. Don't rush this part. The path from the lobby to the spa building is doing something to you before the massage even begins — the palms, the mountains, the red tile roofs, the pool appearing on your right. This is the Peaks to Pools moment. Let it land.
You have a full hour in the facilities before your massage — dry sauna, steam sauna, a large hot tub. Arrive early enough to use it. Then the massage itself, with staff who are experienced and attentive and know when not to talk. Afterward, two hours of pool access. Lounge chairs, daybeds, cabanas, food and drink service. Watch for the popsicle cart. The afternoon has no agenda beyond this.
If the Colony Palms Spa isn't available
The Parker Palm Springs Yacht Club Spa for a more theatrical experience. Grounded Body Works in North Palm Springs for excellent quality without the resort production. Both are worth knowing about.
2
Then — The Pool
The Colony Palms pool is one of the genuinely beautiful places to be horizontal in Palm Springs. You've earned it twice over — once on the mountain and once on the table. Stay as long as the afternoon allows.
3
The Evening
When the appetite for something more arrives, three dinner experiences are worth the occasion.
Miro's
Elegant in the classical sense. The kind of restaurant that makes a night feel like a memory before it's over. Right for ten years.
4 Saints at the Rowan
A proper steakhouse. Well deserved after a morning in the mountains and an afternoon at the pool. Order something substantial.
Canopy
A wine bar and bistro with warmth to it. More relaxed but still considered. Right for two people who want to keep talking rather than perform a celebration.
Any of the three delivers on the feeling you described. Like you earned something together. Because you did.
